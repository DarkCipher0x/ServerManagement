# DarkCipher ServerManagement 0.3.0-preview.2 (2026-10-20)

The second 0.3.0 preview: Group Policy for (almost) every setting, and an uninstaller that leaves nothing behind. It
upgrades 0.3.0-preview.1 and 0.2.2; the installer's version number is 0.2.4.

## New

### Group Policy for every setting
- **107 settings can be set by policy**, for the computer (`HKLM`) or a user (`HKCU`) under
  `SOFTWARE\Policies\DarkCipher Systems\ServerManagement`. The value name is the setting's name (`OfflineMode`,
  `AccentColor`, `LocalApiPort`…). The computer's value wins over the user's, and both win over admin protection and the
  user's own settings. Accounts, keys, tokens and per-PC state are not policies (each excluded with its reason).
- **ADMX / ADML templates** in every release (`policy\`, and `…-policy-templates.zip`) for the Group Policy editor and
  Intune: *Administrative Templates → DarkCipher Systems → DarkCipher ServerManagement*, in eight categories.
  The full list with types and accepted values: `docs/POLICIES.md` (also in the PDF guide).
- **In the app:** Settings → Security & privacy → **Managed by your organisation** lists each managed setting, its value
  and source, and any policy value it ignored as invalid. A managed setting can't be changed: a change is put back and
  the status bar says why; 🛡 switches it sets are greyed out.

### Uninstall leaves nothing behind
- Always removed: the program, shortcuts, the app's registry key and its temp files.
- **"Remove your data too?"** now offers: the encrypted store (with your Windows Hello key), **your shared library
  membership** (local copies and member keys; the team's share is never touched), the cache and logs, **settings
  enforced on this PC** (admin-protected settings and policy values), and **every Windows user on this PC**.
- **Silent switches:** `REMOVE_DATA`, `REMOVE_LIBRARY`, `REMOVE_CACHE`, `REMOVE_SETTINGS`, `REMOVE_ALLUSERS`, or
  `REMOVE_EVERYTHING=1` for all, e.g. `msiexec /x DarkCipherServerManagement-0.3.0-preview.2-x64.msi /qn REMOVE_EVERYTHING=1`.
  Every install and uninstall switch is in one table in the administrator guide.

## Under the hood
- One catalog (`PolicySettings`) built from the settings themselves; the self-test fails if a setting is neither a
  policy nor excluded with a reason, so new settings can't be forgotten. Templates and `docs/POLICIES.md` are generated
  from it and checked by the self-test. The AI features policy is read through the same catalog.
- The uninstall clean-up is one small class (`Services\Uninstall.cs`), tested against scratch folders and a fake
  registry. Decision record: ADR-040.

## Not tested by hand yet
The templates in the Group Policy editor, and a real uninstall with the new options (the self-test covers the logic,
not the installer).
